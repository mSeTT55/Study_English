# Learning method — living experiment

This is a **practical method under refinement**, not a promise of fluency by any fixed date.

## Evidence-informed ingredients
- **Comprehensible input:** content understood with reasonable support; avoid forcing advanced native-speed videos when too difficult.
- **Active recall:** learner produces language before seeing the model answer.
- **Spacing:** re-test after time has passed rather than only repeating immediately.
- **Interaction and feedback:** conversation creates opportunities to negotiate meaning and repair mistakes.
- **Meaningful output:** retell, ask, explain, give an opinion, troubleshoot or chat with others.
- **Shadowing:** imitation of short authentic phrases for stress, rhythm and pronunciation, with awareness that transfer to spontaneous speaking is not automatic.

Useful reading:
- Spaced second-language practice meta-analysis: https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479
- Interaction and instructed second language acquisition: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/interaction-and-instructed-second-language-acquisition/78A156EE200F744F5978F99BFB073DBE
- Shadowing systematic review: https://doi.org/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827

## Flexible session (initial option, NOT a hard rule)
1. 5–10 min: active review of due weak areas.
2. 10–20 min: short story, graded news, household narration, technical lesson or video.
3. 10–20 min: guided and spontaneous conversation, plus targeted corrections.
4. 5 min: recall one or two chunks, log evidence and decide the next review.

An optional real-world Discord session may replace some guided conversation. Before: rehearse survival phrases. During: have a real conversation. After: describe challenges to the tutor.

## Recommended content difficulty
- Start with material that permits understanding the central message without translating every word.
- A learner-rated scale is more useful than inferring CEFR from a video title alone.
- When content is too hard, shorten the clip, use slower listening, add a few glosses, or select a graded version.
- News must have its date, source and publication URL recorded. Never reuse old results as if they were today's.

## Assessment
Each meaningful attempt logs a skill: recognition, listening, guided production, independent production; a target item; correctness; assistance; short feedback; and date. Listening to a video is exposure, **not automatically a successful test**. Use short delayed re-tests across distinct contexts. The chat should be honest when evidence is insufficient.

## Daily life micro-immersion
Use mundane tasks for 10–30 second self-talk: "I'm turning on my PC"; "I'm making lunch"; "I'm closing the window"; "I'm getting tired"; "I'm thinking about that". Later change tense, negatives, questions, and opinions. Move from prompted repetition toward spontaneous phrasing.

## Technical / hobby immersion
Use Linux, DevOps, programming, data engineering, AI, F1, football, games and general curiosity as meaningful topics. For Portuguese videos, the training task is to paraphrase/explain the concept in English. For English videos, listen first, notice chunks, imitate briefly and summarize. Never attribute an unverified quote to a video.

## Rights and privacy
Keep URLs, metadata, short original study notes, and the learner's own sentences. Do not store complete third-party stories, captions, paid textbook content, or recordings from Discord. Public Git repositories are not private diaries.
